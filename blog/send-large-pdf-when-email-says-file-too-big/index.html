How to Send a Large PDF When Email Says File Too Big

If your email app says a PDF is too large to attach, the problem is usually file size, scanned images, extra pages, large margins, or too many documents merged together. Before sending the file, you can reduce the PDF size, remove unnecessary pages, crop extra space, and make the document easier for the receiver to open.

Why your PDF is too large for email

  • Scanned pages are image-heavy and increase file size.
  • Phone photos are often high resolution.
  • Duplicate or blank pages are included by mistake.
  • Large margins or scanner borders add unnecessary data.
  • Multiple documents were merged without cleanup.
  • Images, diagrams, receipts, reports, or certificates make the PDF heavier.

Step-by-step: make a large PDF easier to email

Step 1: Remove unnecessary pages

  1. Open Delete pages.
  2. Remove blank pages, duplicate scans, old drafts, instruction pages, or unrelated pages.
  3. Download the cleaned PDF.

Step 2: Crop large margins or scanner borders

Use Crop PDF to remove extra white space, scanner edges, table background, or unnecessary borders around scanned pages.

Step 3: Compress the PDF

  1. Open Compress PDF.
  2. Upload the cleaned PDF.
  3. Download the compressed version.
  4. Open the file and check that text, names, dates, signatures, numbers, and images remain readable.

Step 4: If your file is still too large, send it in parts

If the PDF is still too large after compression, create two smaller PDFs manually: make a copy of the PDF and use Delete pages to keep only the first set of pages. Then make another copy and delete the first set to keep the remaining pages. Send both PDFs as Part 1 and Part 2.

Step 5: If your documents are photos, make a clean PDF first

If your documents are still JPG/PNG photos, open Image → PDF, upload the images, arrange them in order, and download one PDF. Then compress the final PDF before emailing.

What to check before sending the email

  • The PDF opens correctly after compression.
  • Important details are readable, including names, dates, amounts, signatures, and ID numbers.
  • No blank or duplicate pages are included.
  • No unrelated pages are inside the file.
  • The filename is simple and clear.
  • The receiver can understand the document order without asking for clarification.

Simple filename examples

Use a simple filename before attaching the PDF. Avoid emojis, brackets, very long names, and special characters like #, %, &, +, /, or ?.

  • documents-final.pdf
  • invoice-package.pdf
  • application-documents.pdf
  • supporting-documents-part-1.pdf
  • supporting-documents-part-2.pdf

When should you send multiple PDFs?

Sending one clean PDF is usually easier for the receiver. But if the file remains too large, sending two smaller PDFs can be better than sending one attachment that fails. Use clear names like part-1 and part-2 so the receiver knows the order.

Final checklist

  • Unnecessary pages removed.
  • Large margins or scanner borders cropped.
  • PDF compressed.
  • Readability checked after compression.
  • Filename is simple and professional.
  • If needed, file is divided into clear Part 1 and Part 2 PDFs.
